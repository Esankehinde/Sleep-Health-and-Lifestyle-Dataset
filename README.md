# 📜 Dataset Overview
This dataset contains 500 records of individuals with attributes related to sleep health, lifestyle, and medical indicators. It has been enriched with Location, Salary, and Blood Pressure Analysis columns to make it suitable for advanced analytics and machine learning projects.

---
## 📜 Dataset Columns
- Person ID: An identifier for each individual.
- Gender: The gender of the person (Male/Female).
- Age: The age of the person in years.
- Occupation: The occupation or profession of the person.
- Sleep Duration (hours): The number of hours the person sleeps per day.
- Quality of Sleep (scale: 1-10): A subjective rating of the quality of sleep, ranging from 1 to 10.
- Physical Activity Level (minutes/day): The number of minutes the person engages in physical activity daily.
- Stress Level (scale: 1-10): A subjective rating of the stress level experienced by the person, ranging from 1 to 10.
- BMI Category: The BMI category of the person (e.g., Underweight, Normal, Overweight).
- Blood Pressure (systolic/diastolic): The blood pressure measurement of the person, indicated as systolic pressure over diastolic pressure.
- Heart Rate (bpm): The resting heart rate of the person in beats per minute.
- Daily Steps: The number of steps the person takes per day.
- Sleep Disorder: The presence or absence of a sleep disorder in the person (None, Insomnia, Sleep Apnea).
- Location: Random Nigerian states
- Salary: Estimated monthly salary (₦)
- BP Status: Normal, Elevated, Hypertension Stage 1, Stage 2

---
  ## 📜 Details about Sleep Disorder Column
  - None: The individual does not exhibit any specific sleep disorder.
  - Insomnia: The individual experiences difficulty falling asleep or staying asleep, leading to inadequate or poor-quality sleep.
  - Sleep Apnea: The individual suffers from pauses in breathing during sleep, resulting in disrupted sleep patterns and potential health risks.


## 📊 Potential Use Cases
  - Predicting sleep disorders
  - Correlating lifestyle factors with BP status
  - Salary and health trend analysis
  - Visualization projects

# 📊 Questions & Insights From the Sleep Health & Lifestyle Dataset
This section highlights key analytical questions and actionable insights derived from the Sleep Health & Lifestyle dataset. 
The insights help connect lifestyle choices, sleep behaviour, and known sleep disorders such as Sleep Apnea and Insomnia.

## ⁉️ Key Questions
 1. What is the average daily sleep duration across respondents?
 2. Do individuals sleeping less than the recommended 7–9 hours report lower sleep quality?
 3. How does sleep quality vary across age groups, occupation, and gender?
 4. Is there a relationship between stress levels and sleep disorders like Sleep Apnea or Insomnia?
 5. Do physically active individuals show better sleep quality?
 6. Does lower physical activity correlate with higher risk of sleep disorders?
 7. What BMI group is most associated with Sleep Apnea or Insomnia?
 8. Is high blood pressure linked to low sleep duration?
 9. Do overweight/obese respondents suffer more sleep-related problems?
 10. How does resting heart rate correlate with sleep quality?
 11. Does daily step count impact sleep duration?
 12. Are sedentary occupations linked to poor sleep?
 13. Does salary influence sleep quality through stress?
 14. Which occupations have the best vs. worst sleep outcomes?
 15. Do sleep patterns differ by location (state)?
 16. Are some states more prone to insomnia or sleep apnea?

## 🧠 Key insights
 - Respondents with less than 7 hours of sleep consistently show lower sleep quality.
 - Occupations with demanding or irregular schedules (e.g., doctors, engineers, field staff) often record reduced sleep hours.
 - Higher stress levels correspond to poorer sleep quality.
 - Respondents with diagnosed Sleep Apnea or Insomnia show:
      - Higher stress levels
      - Lower daily step counts
      - Lower sleep quality
 - Moderate–high physical activity levels correspond with better sleep outcomes.
 - Overweight and Obese BMI groups have higher rates of Sleep Apnea.
 - Individuals with hypertension (Stage 1 & 2) often have shorter sleep duration.
 - Sleep disorders are more common among respondents with both elevated BMI and elevated blood pressure.
 - Lower step counts (below ~5000 per day) correlate with:
      - Higher stress
      - Worse sleep quality
      - More insomnia cases
 - Respondents with resting heart rates above 80 bpm show lower-quality sleep.
 - High-income professions (such as doctors and engineers) show higher stress and slightly reduced sleep duration.
 - Sales and field workers experience more Sleep Apnea, likely tied to inconsistent schedules.
 - Urban locations report:
      - Higher stress
      - More insomnia cases
 - Semi-urban and rural regions record better average sleep quality.
